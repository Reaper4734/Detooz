# Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline

## The Problem with Raw Datasets
Machine learning models are only as good as the data they are trained on. When building the training dataset for Detooz, developers utilized standard public datasets (like the UCI Spam Corpus and HuggingFace SMS collections). 

However, a critical gap was identified: these datasets primarily contain older, generic spam ("Buy Viagra", "Click here for mortgage"). They severely lacked modern, highly sophisticated **psychological manipulation, localized Indian scams, and spear-phishing attacks**.

## The Solution: AI-Driven Data Augmentation
To ensure the MobileBERT model could catch modern threats, the Detooz team built a **Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline**. This pipeline uses a massively powerful Large Language Model (Llama 3.3 70B Versatile via Groq API) to invent novel, highly realistic scam scenarios and safely inject them into the training set.

## The Pipeline Architecture & Workflow
The data preparation process is entirely automated through three key orchestrator files, acting together to form the final dataset:

1. **`data_fetcher.py` (The Gatherer)**: 
   This script automatically downloads the primary dataset from HuggingFace and supplementary datasets from UCI. It normalizes different CSV/TSV formats, standardizing labels into a unified format (`text`, `type`, `source`).
   
2. **`synthetic_gen.py` (The Generator)**: 
   This script connects to the Groq API and prompts the LLM to generate specific categories of highly-targeted Indian SMS messages in bulk (Hinglish and English), returning them as clean JSON arrays to be saved as a synthetic CSV file.
   
3. **`pipeline.py` (The Orchestrator)**: 
   The master script that ties it all together. It first calls the fetcher to get real data, triggers the generator to create synthetic samples, and then merges, cleans, and shuffles everything into a single finalized training dataset.

## Augmented Scenarios & Actual Generated Examples
The generator specifically targets corner-cases and high-risk vectors. Below are actual examples generated by our pipeline to train the MobileBERT model:

1. **Traffic E-Challan Scams**
   * *Actual Generated Value:* "Sir ji, your pending traffic e-challan of Rs. 15000 is due, pay at Canara Bank to avoid court in Kolkata."

2. **Emotional Manipulation / Family Distress**
   * *Actual Generated Value:* "Dad, my phone is LOST, plz send 10,000 rs urgently to my SBI ac, i m in pune"

3. **Digital Arrest / Customs Scams**
   * *Actual Generated Value:* "Sir ji, ur parcel seized by CBI in Kolkata, u have 2 pay 20,000 to get it back jaldi!"

4. **Malicious APK Downloads**
   * *Actual Generated Value:* "Dear Saurav Das, your Axis bank account has been locked! DOWNLOAD THIS APK to unlock and CLAIM 20,000 rupees now!!"

5. **Safe Equivalent Generation (Anti-Bias)**
   * *Why?* If the model only sees the word "bank" or "urgent" in scams, it will become biased and block legitimate alerts. Generating safe equivalents balances the dataset.
   * *Actual Generated Value (HAM):* "Dear Mr. Agarwal, your Zomato order is ready for pickup at our Mumbai outlet. Use code FOOD15 for a 15% discount on your next order!"

## Execution
By injecting these synthetically generated, hyper-realistic scams, the Detooz model is preemptively trained to recognize the psychological *intent* and modern vectors of an attack, even if it has never seen the exact phrasing before in the real world.
